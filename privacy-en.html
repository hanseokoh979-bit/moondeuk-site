Privacy Policy

Last updated: October 15, 2026 · 한국어 · 日本語

Moondeuk reads the location stored in the photos in your gallery to build a travel map, and lets you add songs to those trips. This page lists, without exception, what Moondeuk reads, where it keeps it, and when anything leaves your device. If this English translation and the Korean original differ, the Korean original applies.

Moondeuk has no servers. The developer keeps none of your data.

Your original photos never leave your device. They are not included in backups either. Only when you share a ticket does one image newly drawn from that photo go to the app you choose — not the original file, and not its location (section 3 ④).

No ads or analytics tools. No data is passed to any third party.

Data leaves your device in exactly four cases, all listed in section 3 below.

Want to delete your data? Section 6 has a table of how to delete it →

Moondeuk never moves or deletes photos. It only reads them.

2. Where your data is stored

By default, on this device. It is stored only in the app's internal storage, which other apps cannot read.

Only if you turn on backup, one more copy of your travel records is stored in your own Google Drive. It goes to the "app data folder", a hidden area reserved for the app, so it does not appear in your Drive and other apps cannot read it. Neither can the developer.

  • Included — trips, cities, and song links; reference keys used to find your photos again (values made from capture time and coordinates); flags for hidden items; and your home area radius setting
  • Not included — original photos

3. When data leaves your device — only four cases

① Finding place names (everyone)

Coordinates alone don't say "Busan" or "Kyoto", so Moondeuk asks the Android operating system's location service about representative coordinates.

  • It does not ask about each photo — only one or two representative coordinates per group of trip photos
  • The coordinates of the places that Exclude home area in Settings identified around your home are also asked about, once per place — to show you a neighborhood name such as "Sanggye-dong". A name, once received, is kept on the device and not asked again
  • If you open "Is your home missing?" there, the coordinates of the places listed on that screen (places where your photos piled up over several days) are also asked about — to show the places you can choose by neighborhood name. These names are not kept, so they are asked again each time you open that screen
  • No photos are sent
  • This is handled by Android, under Google's privacy policy

② Backup (only if you turn it on)

The contents described in section 2 are sent to your own Google Drive. This includes the coordinates of places you traveled. The app talks to Drive directly, never through a developer server.

Backups are not uploaded automatically. One is uploaded each time you tap in Settings › Data › Backup — it is not a switch that keeps uploading once turned on.

③ Going to listen to a song

Tapping a song opens the music app you chose (Melon, YouTube Music, or Spotify). At that moment the song title and artist are passed to that app as a search term. Moondeuk does not play music itself.

④ Sharing a ticket (only when you tap it)

When you tap Share on the ticket that appears when you tap a photo in the Globe tab, a newly drawn ticket image made from that one photo is handed to Android's share sheet, and the app you choose (a messenger, a social app, saving, and so on) receives it.

  • What the image contains — the photo, the city and country names, the trip's route (names of cities visited) and dates, and the day the photo was taken
  • No photo location (GPS) or other capture metadata from the original is included. It is a newly drawn image, not the original file. The capture time is shown only to the day
  • Moondeuk does not send it anywhere itself. The receiving app is briefly given permission to read only that one image
  • The image stays in the app's temporary folder for a while, and the next time you share, images more than a day old are deleted. Android may also delete it first if your phone runs low on space
  • How the receiving app handles the image follows that app's policy

Nothing other than these four leaves your device. Nothing is sent to a developer server under any circumstances.

4. About notification access

Moondeuk can use notification access for notifications like "You heard this song in Kyoto". The permission has an alarming name, so here is clearly what it does and what it doesn't.

What it does

  • Reads only the title and artist of the song currently playing

What it doesn't do

  • It does not read a single character of notification text. The app's function for receiving notifications is empty
  • It keeps no list, times, or counts of what you listened to. What it reads is compared with the songs you added, then discarded
  • It sends nothing anywhere

The only things kept on the device are what you registered yourself by tapping on screen — a song's spelling you confirmed with "Same as this song", and a song you added by tapping it under "Now playing".

The app works normally without this permission. It is not requested on first launch, and you can turn it off in Settings at any time.

5. Third parties

None.

  • Moondeuk contains no advertising SDKs, analytics tools, or crash reporters
  • Data is not sold or shared
  • Cases ①–④ in section 3 go to your device, your account, or apps you choose; the developer never receives your data

6. Retention and deletion

Data on your device is kept until you delete it. The developer keeps no data, so there is nothing to delete on the developer's side.

WhatHow
All records in the appSettings › Data › Delete all records
Images made for sharingDeleted at your next share once they are a day old. To delete them right away: System Settings → Apps → Moondeuk → Storage → Clear cache
Your backup on DriveGoogle Account → Data & privacy → App data
Photo accessSystem Settings → Apps → Moondeuk → Permissions
Notification accessSystem Settings → Notification access

Deleting the app also deletes the data on the device. Your backup on Drive must be deleted separately as above.

There is no retention period after deletion. The developer keeps no data at all, so once you delete it, it is gone. A backup copy exists only in your own Drive, so deleting it leaves nothing behind.
